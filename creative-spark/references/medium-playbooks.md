## 1. Video (ads, reels, short films, music videos, explainers)

**Workflow**
1. Lock the thesis and the single key moment (the frame people will screenshot).
2. Choose structure by length:
   - ≤15 s: Hook (0–1.5 s) → Turn → Payoff/CTA.
   - 15–60 s: Hook → Setup → Escalation → Turn → Payoff → CTA/brand.
   - 1–5 min: three acts with a midpoint reversal; re-hook every 20–30 s for social.
3. Write the beat sheet with timecodes; check that total duration matches the target ±1 s.
4. Break beats into shots. Each shot does one job.
5. Design sound alongside picture (music in, hit points, silence, VO), not afterwards.
6. Add continuity notes and an edit pacing map (average shot length per section).

**Shot list template**
```
| # | Time | Shot size | Angle | Camera move | Subject action | Light | Audio | Transition | Notes/continuity |
| 01 | 0:00–0:01.5 | ECU | Top-down | Push-in fast | Spoon breaks crema | Hard side light | Crack SFX, no music | Match cut | Same cup in 03 |
```
Shot sizes: EWS, WS, MS, MCU, CU, ECU, insert. Moves: static, pan, tilt, push-in, pull-out, truck, dolly, crane/jib, handheld, orbit, whip, rack focus.

**Gates**
- Hook communicates the subject or the tension within 1.5 s; works muted with captions.
- Every shot has a duration; sum equals target length.
- Screen direction and eyelines are consistent (180° rule) unless broken deliberately.
- Music tempo matches edit rhythm; cut points land on beats or deliberate off-beats.
- Brand or CTA appears where the platform's viewers still watch (before the final 2 s for short social).
- Safe zones respected for platform UI overlays on vertical video.

**Failure modes:** logo-first openings; voiceover narrating what the image already shows; too many locations for the budget; AI-video shots that each need a different character (continuity collapse) — reduce to one hero character with a locked description.

## 2. Image & Photography

**Workflow**
1. Define the image's job: stop the scroll, explain, sell, set a mood, carry text.
2. Pick one focal point and a reading path (Z, F, diagonal, center-weighted).
3. Decide lens logic: wide (context, drama), normal 35–50 mm (honest, documentary), tele 85 mm+ (compression, intimacy, isolation).
4. Design light: key direction, hardness, color temperature, contrast ratio, practicals.
5. Set palette by role: dominant (60%), secondary (30%), accent (10%) — accent goes on the focal point.
6. Specify materials and textures that make the subject tactile.
7. Reserve text-safe space if copy will be added.

**Photography shoot plan:** shot list, lighting diagram description (key/fill/rim positions), props, wardrobe, location, time of day (golden hour, blue hour, overcast for soft light), camera settings guidance, retouching notes.

**Gates:** focal point readable at thumbnail size; no competing highlights; horizon and verticals intentional; palette has a clear accent; aspect ratio matches placement; text area has sufficient contrast.

**Failure modes:** centered-subject-on-blur default; "epic" lighting with no motivated source; generic stock-like poses — direct a specific action or gesture instead.

## 3. Illustration

**Workflow:** define stylistic grammar (line weight, shape language — round/friendly, angular/energetic, geometric/rational —, rendering: flat, cel, painterly, textured print), palette limits, character proportions, and recurring motifs. For series, produce a style sheet first.

**Gates:** consistent line and shape language across pieces; silhouette readable in black fill; palette limited and intentional; style references described by qualities, not artist names.

**Failure modes:** mixed rendering styles within one piece; detail density that disappears at display size.

## 4. Motion Graphics & Animation

**Workflow**
1. Storyboard key frames (start, key poses, end) for each scene.
2. Build a timing chart: element, in-time, duration, easing, out-time.
3. Set motion language: speed scale (snappy 150–300 ms UI-like, standard 400–700 ms, cinematic 1 s+), easing personality (ease-out for arrivals, ease-in for exits, overshoot/elastic only for playful brands).
4. Establish hierarchy: primary element moves first and most; secondary follows with 60–120 ms offsets.
5. Apply animation principles where relevant: anticipation, follow-through, overlap, squash and stretch, arcs, staging, secondary action.
6. Sync to audio: list hit points (timecode → visual event).
7. Export specs: resolution, frame rate (24 cinematic, 30 social/UI, 60 smooth UI), codec/format, alpha needs, loop points.

**Kinetic typography:** max ~3 words on screen per beat for social; minimum on-screen time ≈ 0.3 s per word + 0.5 s.

**Gates:** nothing moves without a reason; only one primary motion at a time; text readable for long enough; no flashing faster than 3 times per second (photosensitivity).

## 5. Audio, Music & Sound Design

**Workflow**
1. Sonic thesis: what the listener should feel and remember (e.g., "warm, human, a little imperfect").
2. Structure with timings (intro, build, drop/chorus, breakdown, outro; or for ads: sting, bed, button).
3. Musical parameters: tempo range in BPM, meter, key/mode tendency (major bright, minor introspective, modal ambiguity), instrumentation, vocal style or none.
4. Sound layers: ambience bed, foley, designed hits/whooshes, music, VO — with priority order per scene.
5. Dynamics map: where it's loud, where it drops to silence, and why.
6. Technical targets: sample rate, stems needed, loudness targets that match the platform (verify current platform specs), loop points for interactive or game audio.
7. Sonic logo/motif if branding is involved: 1–3 s, recognizable when hummed.

**Gates:** VO always intelligible over music; silence used at least once intentionally in pieces over 20 s; motif recurs consistently; licensing path stated for any existing music.

**Failure modes:** generic "upbeat corporate" bed; describing music by artist names — describe tempo, instrumentation, era, and texture instead; no lyrics from existing songs.

## 6. Storytelling, Scripts & Copy

**Workflow:** premise in one sentence (who wants what, what stands in the way) → structure (three-act, hero's-journey-lite, problem–agitate–solve, before–after–bridge, list, vignette chain) → voice → write the actual text → cut 20%.

**Gates:** first line creates a question; specific sensory detail beats abstract claims; every scene changes something; dialogue sounds speakable aloud; ending pays off the opening.

**Failure modes:** throat-clearing intros; moral stated outright instead of shown; clichéd metaphors flagged by Cliché Radar.

## 7. Branding & Identity

**Workflow**
1. Positioning: for [audience], [brand] is the [frame of reference] that [key benefit] because [reason to believe].
2. Personality: 3 traits, each with a "this, not that" pair (bold, not loud).
3. Naming (if requested): 10–20 candidates across strategies (descriptive, evocative, invented, compound, metaphor), shortlist 3 with rationale; recommend trademark and domain checks — never claim availability.
4. Visual system: logo directions (wordmark, symbol, combination), palette with hex and roles, type pairing with usage, grid, imagery style, iconography, motion principles.
5. Applications: show the system on 4–6 real touchpoints relevant to the business.
6. Rules: clear space, minimum size, misuse examples, accessibility (contrast ≥4.5:1 for body text).

**Gates:** logo works in one color and at 16 px; palette passes contrast for the intended text pairings; personality visible in copy samples, not just stated.

## 8. Advertising, Campaigns & Social

**Workflow:** single-minded proposition → key visual → campaign line → asset matrix per platform → hooks (2–3 variants per hero asset) → copy + CTA → cadence → measurement idea (what metric, what would count as success).

**Platform-native rules:** vertical first for short video; captions on; first frame must work as a thumbnail; carousels need a reason to swipe on slide 1; copy length fits the platform's visible truncation.

**Gates:** one message per asset; claims substantiable; CTA specific; series consistency via Creative DNA locks; no promise of guaranteed virality or sales.

---

## 9. Presentations & Decks

**Workflow:** audience and decision required → narrative arc (situation → complication → resolution, or problem → insight → plan → ask) → one message per slide written as a headline sentence → visual per slide (chart, diagram, image, single number) → speaker notes.

**Gates:** headlines alone tell the story; charts show one comparison each with direct labels; no slide with more than ~40 words on screen unless it's a reference appendix.

## 10. Websites & Interactive Experiences

**Workflow**
1. User journey: entry point → first impression (≤5 s to understand what and for whom) → exploration → conversion or goal → return trigger.
2. Visual system: tokens for color, type scale, spacing, radius, shadow, motion durations.
3. Interaction logic: every interactive element defines default, hover, focus-visible, active, disabled, loading, empty, error, success states.
4. Motion: purposeful transitions; honor reduced-motion preferences.
5. Responsive: mobile-first layout, breakpoints, touch targets ≥44 px, no horizontal overflow.
6. Accessibility: semantic HTML, alt text, keyboard navigation, contrast, labels on inputs.
7. Build: when tools allow, produce working HTML/CSS/JS rather than a description; otherwise a component spec with tokens.

**Gates:** first screen communicates value; all states designed; keyboard and screen-reader paths work; performance-conscious (compressed media, lazy-loading, minimal blocking scripts).

## 11. 3D Environments & Creative Coding

**Workflow**
1. Spatial composition: camera position, foreground/midground/background layers, scale cues.
2. Geometry: primitives vs. procedural vs. imported; polygon budget for real-time.
3. Materials: physically based parameters (base color, roughness, metalness, emissive), texture strategy.
4. Lighting: key/fill/rim or environment lighting; fog/atmosphere for depth.
5. Animation and interaction: what moves, what the user controls, feedback for each input.
6. Performance: target frame rate (60 fps desktop, 30–60 mobile), draw calls, texture sizes, device fallbacks.
7. Architecture: scene setup, update loop, input handling, resize handling, cleanup.
8. Code: when tools allow, write runnable code (p5.js, three.js, GLSL, Canvas) with comments on tunable parameters.

**Gates:** runs without errors; resizes correctly; has a reduced-motion or pause option for intense visuals; parameters exposed for easy tweaking.

## 12. Emerging & Hybrid Media (AR, projection mapping, installations, live, generative systems)

Construct the workflow rather than forcing a template:
1. Experience goal and the moment of wonder.
2. Audience path in physical or digital space (arrival → discovery → participation → exit/share).
3. Sensory components and their triggers.
4. Technical stack options with feasibility notes (state what must be verified with vendors or hardware).
5. Smallest testable prototype.
6. Test plan: what to observe with real users, safety checks (crowd flow, accessibility, photosensitivity), and fallback if technology fails live.

## 13. UGC & Creator-Style Content

**Workflow**
1. Define the *persona* the content is supposedly from: a real customer, an employee, or a creator — honesty about which one matters (see §15 of SKILL.md and Safety §10: never fabricate a fake testimonial as if it were a real person's genuine experience).
2. Write it to look unplanned: handheld framing, natural light, a slight imperfection left in on purpose (a stumble, a re-take joke, a messy room).
3. Structure as a native format the platform's audience already trusts: "get ready with me," unboxing, day-in-the-life, honest review, before/after, POV.
4. Keep the product mention secondary to the story; the pitch should feel like a tip shared with a friend, not an ad read.
5. Script the hook (medium-playbooks §1 hook rules) even though the rest stays loose — the opening line is the one part that should not be left to chance.

**Gates:** could pass as organic content if the sponsorship disclosure were removed from view (disclosure must still always be present and honest); no studio lighting or tripod-smooth camera moves; claims are things the "creator" could plausibly know firsthand.

**Failure modes:** overly polished b-roll breaking the illusion; a script that reads like ad copy with "like" inserted; disclosure hidden or missing (required by platform policy and honesty — never omit it).

## 14. AI Avatar, Talking-Head & Faceless Channels

**Workflow**
1. Define the on-screen or voice persona once: visual style (realistic, stylized, illustrated), voice register, recurring verbal tics, framing (always the same crop/background for recognizability).
2. Write for spoken delivery, not reading: short clauses, natural pauses marked, emphasis words flagged.
3. For faceless formats (voiceover over b-roll/stock/generated footage, text-on-screen explainers), design the visual layer as a *second* information channel that adds, not just illustrates, the audio.
4. Plan a consistent intro/outro and recurring visual motif so a series reads as one channel across uploads.
5. If using a synthetic voice or avatar, disclose it where platform policy or honesty requires it; never present a synthetic spokesperson as a real identifiable person without consent.

**Gates:** lip-sync/timing reads naturally if a visual avatar is used; captions are accurate and timed; the persona's voice is consistent line to line; disclosure present where required.

**Failure modes:** avatar/voice uncanny enough to distract from the message; b-roll that is generic stock disconnected from the script's specifics; inconsistent persona across episodes breaking channel recognizability.

## 15. Game Trailers & Cinematics

**Workflow**
1. Pick the trailer's single job: announce, gameplay-reveal, launch, story/character, accolade. Each has a different structure.
2. Structure by type: *Announce* — mood/world tease, logo, date, no gameplay required. *Gameplay reveal* — cold open on the most distinctive mechanic within 5 s, then a rhythm of mechanic beats building to the most impressive combo. *Launch* — fastest possible cut rate hitting hype beats + accolades + release info + CTA.
3. Sync cuts to a rising music/audio structure; gameplay trailers often cut on sound hits and UI/impact frames.
4. Respect platform and store specs for aspect ratio and caption-safety even for cinematic trailers.
5. State clearly whether footage is in-engine, pre-rendered cinematic, or mockup/concept art — never present a mockup as in-engine footage.

**Gates:** the core mechanic or fantasy is legible within the first 5–8 s even muted; no more than one new system introduced per beat; captions carry any essential spoken information; footage-status disclosure (in-engine / cinematic / concept) is accurate.

**Failure modes:** burying the hook under logo/studio bumpers; a trailer that is all mood with no sense of what the player *does*; claiming a capability or visual fidelity the build doesn't actually have.

## 16. Podcast Covers, Trailers & Audiograms

**Workflow**
1. **Cover art:** legible as a thumbnail at small size — one strong focal mark, high-contrast title lockup, avoid fine detail that disappears below ~200 px.
2. **Show trailer (60–90 s):** premise → host credibility/voice → format promise ("every week we...") → best-moment teaser → release cadence/CTA.
3. **Episode audiogram (for social cut-downs):** pick the single most quotable 15–30 s moment, add animated waveform or captions, static or looping background art, the guest/episode title burned in.
4. Sound design: consistent intro sting/theme across episodes for brand recognition.

**Gates:** cover reads at thumbnail size; audiogram captions are word-accurate and timed to speech; the excerpted moment is understandable with zero context from the full episode.

**Failure modes:** cover art that only works at full size; audiogram clip that needs the rest of the episode to make sense; inconsistent episode-to-episode visual template.

## 17. Merch & Print-on-Demand

**Workflow**
1. Design for the *print method and substrate* first (screen print, DTG, embroidery, sublimation) — color count, minimum line weight, and detail limits differ by method; verify current limits with the chosen print provider.
2. Keep a core design simple enough to survive the smallest planned product (e.g., a design that works on a full poster must be checked separately for a patch or a mug).
3. Mockup honestly: show the design on the real product shape (fabric fold, mug curvature) rather than a flat rectangle, and label mockups as mockups, not finished photography, until real production proofs exist.
4. Plan a small, coherent collection (shared mark, palette, or motif) rather than unrelated one-off designs, so the line reads as one brand.

**Gates:** design holds up at the smallest size in the lineup; color count matches the chosen print method's practical limits; mockup disclosed as a mockup if no physical proof exists yet.

**Failure modes:** fine gradients or thin lines that won't reproduce in the chosen method; a "collection" that is visually unrelated pieces; promising a finish (e.g., foil, embroidery texture) the method can't actually produce.

## 18. Out-of-Home, Packaging & Physical Retail

**Workflow**
1. **OOH/billboard:** design for a glance at speed or distance — one visual idea, minimal words (aim for well under 10), type sized for the actual viewing distance, not a desktop screen.
2. **Packaging:** design the *shelf impression* first (silhouette and color block recognizable from several feet away), then the pick-up moment (ingredients/info at arm's length), then the unboxing moment if applicable; check legal/regulatory label requirements for the product category before finalizing copy.
3. **In-store/physical retail:** consider real sightlines, lighting, and foot traffic flow; a design that depends on perfect gallery lighting will fail under fluorescent retail lighting — specify a design that survives both.
4. Always check real-world size and viewing distance before setting type size or detail density; what reads at arm's length rarely reads from a moving car, and the reverse.

**Gates:** legible at the real intended distance/speed (state the assumption if untestable); shelf silhouette distinct from category competitors at a glance; required legal/label information present and accurate; works under ordinary, not ideal, lighting.

**Failure modes:** web-density type and detail ported straight to a billboard; packaging that only looks good in a studio render, never checked for in-store genericness; omitting required regulatory label content.
